# Stratego Projects

This repository contains two independently playable Godot projects on the same `main` branch.

## Classic

`classic/` is the stable version: 2–4 players, trainable bots, configurable Scout movement, four-square fog of war, and optional private battle results. New gameplay experiments should not be developed here, although important fixes can still be applied deliberately.

Use **Play Classic.bat** to launch it. The original **Play Stratego.bat**, **Train Stratego Bot.bat**, and **Evaluate Stratego Bot.bat** launch Classic for backward compatibility.

## New

`new/` is the substantially different WEGO formation prototype. Players plan full paths simultaneously; movement resolves by weight-speed impulses, followed by melee/retreat batches, ranged fire, leftover movement, and end-of-round victory checks. It includes the bridge-crossing scenario and retains a four-player fog mode.

Use **Play New.bat** to launch it. New has a separate Godot application identity, so its trained bots and other `user://` data cannot overwrite Classic's data.

## Tests and training

- **Test Classic.bat** and **Test New.bat** run each version's automated checks.
- **Train Classic Bot.bat** and **Evaluate Classic Bot.bat** retain the trained-policy workflow.
- New currently uses a heuristic simultaneous-order bot while the WEGO action space is stabilized; its training/evaluation launchers run bounded self-play diagnostics rather than persisting a champion model.

The source folders are self-contained Godot projects. Open `classic/project.godot` or `new/project.godot` in the editor, rather than opening the repository root as a Godot project.

## Product plan

The game is a turn-based, grid-based tactical wargame with simultaneous movement, hidden information, asymmetric scenarios, and optional AI command layers.

At its core, each player controls an army made of units defined by three things:
- Role: Infantry, Archer, or Cavalry
- Weight: Light, Medium, or Heavy
- Strength: current fighting strength on a 1–10 scale
Role describes battlefield behavior rather than literal equipment. Weight determines mobility and armor. The art can make the units concrete: a “Heavy Cavalry” role might visually be plated shock troops or dismounted men-at-arms rather than literal horsemen.
Unit roles
Infantry
- Gets +3 battle score when defending
- Best at holding ground and receiving attacks
Cavalry
- Gets +3 battle score when attacking
- Best at initiating melee and exploiting gaps
Archers
- No melee score bonus
- Fight in melee normally when they enter an enemy square
- Their special rules apply only during the ranged attack phase
Weight
- Light: Movement 3, Armor 0
- Medium: Movement 2, Armor 1
- Heavy: Movement 1, Armor 2
Strength and mobility are independent. Strength represents how many troops are still fighting fit, their cohesion, effectiveness, etc.
Heavy units are intentionally slow but hard to shift. Light units are more maneuverable and will probably be more expensive later once point values are tuned.
Turn structure
Turns are WEGO: both sides issue orders first, then the engine resolves them simultaneously.
1. Planning
Each unit gets a complete movement path, with one adjacent square for every movement point it plans to use.
The UI shows a ghost path indicating which square the unit occupies on each movement impulse.
Orders that cause two units controlled by the same player to collide are rejected immediately as illegal.
2. Simultaneous movement
Movement resolves in three impulses.
Light units move on impulses 1, 2, and 3. Medium units move on impulses 2 and 3. Heavy units move only on impulse 3.
Movement points are spent for squares actually attempted during resolution, not for the full planned path. Attempting to enter a square still spends the point if the unit subsequently loses or bounces back.
The engine checks collisions during every impulse, not just at final destinations.
This means units can fight even if their final destinations differ.
3. Melee
Enemy units occupying the same space or crossing directly through each other trigger combat.
A unit that deliberately moves into an enemy occupied square counts as attacking. If two enemies cross paths, both count as attackers.
All combats generated by the same impulse resolve simultaneously.
4. Retreats
After all combats in the batch resolve, destroyed units are removed.
Then all retreats resolve simultaneously.
A retreat square that was already occupied before the retreat phase is unavailable.
If two enemy units retreat into the same previously empty square, they fight a special retreat battle. This only happens when both units are retreating into an empty square. You can't retreat into a square occupied by an enemy.
5. Ranged phase
After movement and melee are finished, eligible Archers may shoot.
All ranged attacks resolve simultaneously.
6. Leftover movement
After ranged fire, any unit with unused movement may spend at most 1 remaining movement point.
This phase is also simultaneous.
Entering an enemy square during leftover movement creates a normal melee battle.
Combat
Each participating unit rolls a d10 capped by its current Strength.
Example: a Strength 4 unit effectively rolls:
1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4
Then add role bonuses:
- Cavalry attacking: +3
- Infantry defending: +3
- Archer: no role modifier
A higher battle score wins.
A tie does not count as a defender victory. A highest-score tie across opposing
sides causes a bounce; an allied tie for the best score still wins for that side.
Damage
Every unit deals damage based on its own battle score.
Damage is reduced by the target’s Armor.
If a unit wins the battle, its Armor is doubled for that combat:
- Light winner: Armor 0
- Medium winner: Armor 2
- Heavy winner: Armor 4
A natural 10 on the raw d10 always causes +1 extra damage after armor and all other reductions.
So even a weak unit can occasionally chip a Heavy unit.
Mutual destruction is possible.
Win, loss, and bounce
There are three distinct combat outcomes.
Win
The unit wins the contested space.
Winning ends the unit's remaining main-phase movement path.
If it has movement remaining, it may still shoot during the ranged phase or move during the leftover movement phase.
Loss
The unit retreats.
Its actions are finished for the rest of the round.
If it cannot retreat legally, it is destroyed.
Bounce
Opposing sides tied for the highest score, so nobody wins the space.
Every surviving participant returns to the square it occupied before the collision and is finished for the round.
A bounce is not the same thing as losing.
For an attacker, bounce and loss may put it back on the same square, but for a defender:
- bounce = stays in place
- loss = forced away
Friendly movement congestion also returns formations to their prior squares, but carries no round-status penalty and is skipped during click-through review.
Multi-unit combat
More than two units may participate in the same battle.
For example, two Cavalry units may attack one Infantry unit.
Every participant rolls once.
Each unit takes damage equal to the highest opposing battle score, minus its armor.
Damage from multiple enemies does not stack together.
A single unique highest scorer wins the space.
If two friendly units attack together and one clearly wins:
- the winning unit occupies the square
- the other friendly attacker returns without a status penalty
If two friendly attackers tie each other for the highest score while both beat the enemy:
- the enemy loses and retreats
- both friendly attackers return without a status penalty
- the contested square ends empty
If the highest score is tied across opposing sides:
- nobody wins
- every surviving participant bounces and is finished for the round
Retreat battles
If retreating enemies collide in the same newly available square, they fight a retreat battle.
Retreat battles represent disorganized formations crashing into each other.
There is:
- no attacker
- no defender
- no Infantry bonus
- no Cavalry bonus
The higher score wins.
The loser is destroyed.
A tie destroys both.
There is no second retreat.
Ranged combat
Archers can shoot during the ranged phase if they did not lose or bounce in melee.
Short shot
- Range 1
- Costs 1 unused movement point
Long shot
- Range 2 using Manhattan grid distance
- Only available if the Archer did not actually move during the main phase
- Consumes all of the Archer's movement for the round
This gives Light Archers strong skirmishing ability while letting Heavy Archers act more like static long-range fire support.
All ranged attacks resolve simultaneously.
Focus fire is allowed. If multiple Archers overkill a unit, excess damage is simply lost.
An Archer that lost melee and retreated cannot shoot that turn.
An Archer that won melee may still shoot if it has movement remaining.
Leftover movement
After ranged combat, units with unused movement may spend up to 1 square.
This prevents fast units from intentionally saving most of their movement until after seeing the results of ranged combat.
The leftover phase allows:
- Archers to shoot and reposition
- melee units to close the final square
- a winner to exploit a small opening
- a unit to attack after an Archer softens the target
Normal melee rules apply.
This means a unit can usually fight at most twice in one round:
- once during main movement
- once during leftover movement
If it loses or bounces, it cannot act again that round.
Fog of war and information
The game supports 4 players and fog of war.
Each hidden unit has an immutable internal identity, but enemy identity knowledge only persists while continuous sight is maintained.
The battle log preserves historical facts.
Movement itself can reveal information. If a unit moves 3 squares, the opponent can infer that it is Light.
Combat reveals:
- role
- weight
- current Strength
Allied units still physically block one another.
Different allied players can accidentally collide because they cannot see each other’s secret orders. Such congestion returns the moving units to their prior squares without preventing later actions that round.
Scenario design
The game is scenario-driven rather than centered on total annihilation.
Scenario components can vary independently:
- map
- deployment zones
- public objective
- turn limit
- point cap
- special terrain
Candidate scenario types include:
- Bridge Crossing
- Breakthrough
- Withdrawal
- Meeting Engagement
- Hold the Crossing
- Reconnaissance
- Last Stand
- Raid
- Pursuit
- Relief Force
- Multi-objective battles
Objectives force action and prevent Infantry lines from simply staring at each other indefinitely.
Bridge Crossing scenario
The first major test scenario is a river crossing.
Deployment
Defender
- may deploy anywhere on its side of the river
- may not deploy on the bridge
Attacker
- deploys along its own board edge
The bridge should be roughly 3–4 squares wide so the defender cannot trivially block it with one unit.
Bridge squares are completely normal squares for movement, combat, retreat, etc.
Objective
The attacker wins if, at the end of a round, at least 20 current Strength worth of surviving units are on the defender’s side of the river.
The defender wins if the attacker fails to do this before the turn limit.
The initial test turn limit is 20 turns, subject to playtesting.
Victory is checked at end of round rather than immediately during movement.

AI opponents
The game supports multiple AI layers.
Heuristic tactical bot
A deterministic weighted-heuristic bot can choose legal moves based on factors such as:
- objective progress
- distance
- attack value
- unit survival
- enemy type
- local danger
This bot is intentionally simple and reliable.
LLM tactical general
An LLM can receive a deterministic description of:
- current visible state
- unit stats
- terrain
- legal destinations
- objective
- rules
- turn history
It returns one structured order for every controlled unit.
Invalid LLM order = Hold

The engine should generate a deterministic textual/structured state such as:
- unit IDs
- exact coordinates
- roles
- weights
- Strength
- legal destinations
- terrain
- scenario objective
- turn number
The engine may also provide purely geometric facts, but it should not inject strategic judgments like “this unit is vulnerable” or “this bridge should be defended.”
The LLM should make those strategic conclusions itself.
Battle history and memory
LLM generals need some memory of earlier turns, but longer context is not assumed to be better.
Memory length is a testable parameter.
Configurations:
- current state only
- last turn
- last 3 turns
- last 5 turns
- rolling summary + recent turns
- full history
A frontier model may be able to play 20–50 turns with full history, but compacting older events may improve signal-to-noise and performance.
Turn summaries
Each turn can generate a concise factual summary from the authoritative event log.
The summary should describe what happened without telling the player what they should do.

The game can keep:
- full raw event log for replay/debugging

Deterministic replay/export
The current prototype exports a versioned JSON replay containing the scenario setup and seed, both order phases for every completed round, and the exact combat dice stream. Phase event/state digests and a final-state digest let the loader rerun the authoritative engine and reject altered or divergent recordings. The in-game Replay Last control opens the verified event sequence in the same click-through battle presentation used during play.
