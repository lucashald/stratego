# The Ashmere Line — a chronicle

The running story of the campaign, in-world. Mechanics and debriefs live in
`design_log.md` and each battle's `.md`; this file is the part that's meant to
be read. Rosters are the ledger. This is what the ledger was for.

---

## I. The Toll Road

They came to charge a toll on the Oxfell crossing and left most of a company in
the mud. Vare's people had never met the Ashmere levy before and had no reason
to be careful, and it cost them seven formations to learn otherwise. Ashmere lost
three, and all three were speed — the light archer Kestrel, the light horse Ash,
and Ferrant.

Ferrant is the one they still talk about. Medium cavalry, and in a single
afternoon she fought four separate actions: lost to Reeve, came back and beat
Reeve, beat Toll-taker, beat Tally, and only broke on the fifth exchange, caught
between two enemies at once with Marrow's stand collapsing beside her. That isn't
a formation being destroyed. That's a formation being *outnumbered to death*. Not
everyone who rode under her banner died in the mud. Enough of them got clear that
somewhere there are riders who know exactly how it ended and haven't forgotten.
The banner doesn't come back. The riders might.

Word of the Toll Road reached Ashweir inside a day.

## II. Ashweir Bridge

Vare's garrison commander did not send another toll company. He called up the
muster — eleven formations, heavier than the last, and he had read the reports.
He knew how the Toll Road had gone and he did not intend to fight it twice.

The river runs the whole width of the field at Ashweir. One bridge crosses it,
two spans wide, and Ashmere held the near bank. The order was not to take
anything. The order was to still be standing on the near bank when Vare gave up.

Seven formations answered for Ashmere. Two of them were bows.

That detail decided everything. After the Toll Road, Ashmere had lost every fast,
mounted, and ranged formation it owned, and the levy could only afford one new
recruit before Ashweir. The choice was a fresh troop of light horse or a second
archer. Ashmere took the archer. Her name was Wren, and she had never stood in a
line before Ashweir Bridge.

Here is what two bows do to a two-span gap.

Vare fed the bridge the way you feed a fire that won't catch — one formation into
the gap, then the next, then the next, because a bridge only lets you send what
the bridge is wide enough to hold. And into that gap Harrow and Wren shot all
day. Wren killed Tithe, then Warden, then Bailiff, and put arrows into the Vare
Heavies besides — a recruit who had never loosed in anger emptying three enemy
formations off the field before noon. Harrow, who had done the same work at the
Toll Road, killed the archer Fletch, killed both Vare Heavies outright, finished
what Wren started on Siegewright. Between the two of them, nearly every Vare
formation that died at Ashweir died to an arrow.

They died for it.

Siegewright, before he fell, got one shot back across the water and it was
enough — Wren went down at the bridge she had just spent the morning holding, on
the first day she ever fought. Harrow lasted longer, long enough to see the Vare
line broken, and then Outrider — light infantry, the last fast thing Vare had —
got in among the bows and rode Harrow down in the final exchange of the battle.

Around the archers, the rest of the Line spent itself. Oakhand met Vare's Levy at
the bridge mouth and the two of them destroyed each other in the same instant, a
clean trade, both banners down together. Coldbrook was cut apart by Siegewright
covering the same crossing. Marrow and Thistle — who at the Toll Road had between
them killed the Toll-taker that broke Ferrant — were shot off the bank by Tithe
before the fight even closed, the two of them gone early and without a melee to
their names. Marrow got clear of the Toll Road only to not get clear of Ashweir.

And Stonewatch stood on the bridge.

Stonewatch is Heavy Infantry, Strength 8, and at the Toll Road she never fought
at all — the battle simply never reached her side of the line, and she came home
without a story. At Ashweir she got exactly one fight: Picket, Vare light
infantry, tried to force the span she was standing on, and Stonewatch put them in
the river. That was the whole of it. One crossing, one enemy in the water, and
then she stood at the bridgehead and did not move and did not fall while every
other formation in the Ashmere Line died around her, one after another, until
there were none of them left and the bridge was just her.

Vare's crossing broke somewhere in the long middle of the battle and never
re-formed. Their commander was left with two formations that could not force
anything — Bulwark, a Heavy that had never once closed, and Outrider, bled down
to a third of itself. The clock ran the rest of the way out with nothing left on
either side willing or able to decide it. When the last round passed, Ashmere
held the near bank of the river at Ashweir.

They held it with one formation.

## III. After the bridge

The tally, plainly. Ashmere went to Ashweir with seven and holds one: Stonewatch.
Vare came with eleven and keeps two. Nineteen formations stood at first light and
three were standing at dusk, and Ashmere's one is worth more than Vare's two,
because Ashmere's one is standing on the objective.

It is a victory. It is also the end of the Ashmere Line as an army. You cannot
call a single Heavy Infantry formation a line. What's left is a survivor, a
reputation, and a river that Vare now knows costs more to cross than it looks.

**The fallen at Ashweir, struck from the roster and not returning:**

- *Harrow* (Mounted Archer). Two battles, and in both of them the deadliest thing
  Ashmere fielded. Killed more of Vare than any other formation in the campaign.
  Ridden down at the end, with the line already broken in front of him.
- *Wren* (Light Archer). One battle. Three enemy formations killed in it. The
  recruit taken instead of cavalry, who justified the entire choice before she
  died on the first day she ever fought.
- *Oakhand* (Heavy Infantry). Traded itself square for square with Vare's Levy —
  neither walked away.
- *Coldbrook* (Light Infantry). Two battles without a wound at the Toll Road; cut
  down holding the bridge mouth at Ashweir.
- *Marrow* (Medium Infantry). Survived being caught beside Ferrant at the Toll
  Road; shot off the bank early at Ashweir.
- *Thistle* (Medium Infantry). Killed the Toll-taker and beat Scrip back to back
  at the Toll Road — the two hardest fights of that battle. Gone to an arrow at
  Ashweir before the lines even met.

**The survivor:**

- *Stonewatch* (Heavy Infantry, Strength 8, unwounded). Two battles now. In the
  first she never fought; in the second she outlived an entire army on a bridge.
  She has earned the name literally: she is the stone that watched. Whatever
  Ashmere rebuilds, it rebuilds around her — and she carries out of Ashweir the
  thing veterans earn (see `roster.json`): the right, once a battle, to refuse a
  single bad roll of the dice and demand another. She held the bridge alone. The
  dice can give her that.

**Loose on the field, and worth remembering:**

- *Outrider* (Vare light infantry) rode away from Ashweir at a third strength,
  and rode away having personally killed Harrow. A face that could come back —
  the same kind of open thread as Ferrant's riders, pointed the other direction.
- *Bulwark* (Vare Heavy) never struck a blow at Ashweir and carried word of it
  back to the garrison intact. Vare has now lost badly to Ashmere twice. The next
  thing they send will not be a muster. It will be something that has stopped
  trying to win the crossing the honest way.

The Line is one formation and a story. Ashmere has to become an army again before
Vare comes back, and it cannot do it the way it did before — there isn't enough
left to lose a fair fight and recover. What comes next is not another bridge.
