# Dispatch 01 — Cassewick

Campaign turn 1. Treasury: 15 crowns.

## Intelligence

Vare's tide did not stop at the broken Ashmere frontier. A fast column has crossed
into Halgate's sphere ahead of the main host, and it is riding for **Cassewick** —
the granary town on the road to the city, where Halgate keeps its winter stores.
Take Cassewick and Vare either burns the stores to starve the city or holds the
town to open the road south. Halgate cannot let either happen.

**Known enemy force — a raiding vanguard, no heavies.** Fast, built to seize the
town before Halgate's slow wall can arrive:

- **Warrant** — Light Cavalry (6). The rider that ran the last of Ashmere down on
  the Fen Road. Leads the column.
- **Escheat** — Medium Cavalry (7). The lance of the vanguard.
- **Distress** — Light Infantry (6).
- **Summons** — Light Infantry (6).
- **Quarrel** — Light Archer (5). The bow Ashmere had no answer to. Halgate does.

Five formations, all speed and reach, no formation that wants to stand and slug.
Their whole plan is to be at Cassewick before you are.

## The decision

Send one company. The other stays. This is the choice that means a lost battle
costs a company, not Halgate.

**Send the Charter Company (fast).** Match their speed. Race them to Cassewick and
contest the town on even mobility — cavalry against cavalry, skirmisher against
skirmisher, on ground where getting there first is half the battle. It is the
natural counter to a raid. The risk is that it is a genuinely even fight and your
formations run a little thinner than the Guard's; a mobile battle can swing.

**Send the Gate Guard (wall).** Plant the heavies and the heavy bows in Cassewick
and a light vanguard cannot dig them out — no formation in Vare's column wants to
charge Portcullis and Sable. The risk is the whole reason Vare sent horse: the
Guard is slow. Vare may reach the stores first, or simply decline the wall and
work around it to the objective while your heavies are still on the road. Holding
a town you have not yet reached is not holding it.

Speed to contest it, or a wall to deny it — against an enemy whose entire design
is to be somewhere before you can be.
