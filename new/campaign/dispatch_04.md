# Dispatch 04 — the lull, and the problem

Campaign turn 4. Treasury: 8 crowns (and +8 each turn it isn't spent).

## Where things stand

The board, plainly:

- **The Weirgate held; Vare's heavy host is destroyed.** The road to the city is
  shut and the main threat is gone. Vare is regrouping, not attacking — there is a
  lull, and it is yours to use.
- **Cassewick is still Vare's.** Two attempts to take it back both won the fighting
  and lost the town. The garrison there is thin (Forfeit and Chattel limped out of
  the last fight) but it holds.
- **Your armies are lopsided.** The **Gate Guard** is near-whole, unbeaten, and the
  only real force you have — but slow, built to hold, not to take. The **Charter
  Company** is two Medium Archers (Vellum, Ledger): fast and sharp, but it has
  proven twice it cannot seize and hold anything on its own.

The lesson the war has taught, in one line: **Halgate can defend, but it cannot yet
take ground, because everything it has sent to take ground has been unable to hold
it.** Winning the fighting isn't the problem. Standing on the objective afterwards
is.

## The decision — how do you fight this?

This one is strategic, not a single deployment. Pick a direction and I build the
turn around it:

- **A. Consolidate and build a real strike force.** Take the lull. Bank a turn or
  two of income and rebuild toward a *combined-arms* company — infantry or a heavy
  to hold the ground your bows keep winning, archers behind it. Accept that
  Cassewick stays Vare's a while longer, and come back able to actually take it.
  (Slow, correct, cedes tempo.)
- **B. Send the Gate Guard to take Cassewick — as a grind, not a race.** The Guard
  can hold anything it reaches; the trick is a scenario where slow doesn't lose,
  i.e. an assault with no relief clock, where the wall simply walks up and stays.
  Commits your one good army to an offensive, on ground chosen to suit it.
- **C. Stay on the defensive and let Vare come to the wall again.** Play to the one
  thing that has worked. Bank treasury, keep the Guard fresh, and win the next
  Weirgate. (Safe, but it never takes anything back — a war you don't lose and
  don't win.)

And the standing offer holds: for any battle where you want a real opponent instead
of the passive bot, **I can command Vare myself.**

Tell me the direction (and, if B, whether to reinforce/hire from the 8 crowns), and
I'll build the turn — verified before it reaches you, as always.
